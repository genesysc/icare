What is live-in care?

Live-in care means a carer lives in the client's home for an agreed period, providing support over the course of the day and night. It covers personal care, companionship, medication prompts, and help around the home, and it suits people who need continuous support but want to stay where they are.

  • Carers usually work set blocks, such as one or two weeks, followed by agreed time off.
  • Accommodation, meals and sleeping arrangements are agreed with the employer before a block starts.
  • Pay is usually agreed as a day rate, a weekly rate, or a 24-hour rate.

Questions carers ask

Does iCare rank or score carers?

No. Profiles aren't scored, ranked or sorted by rate. Employers search by the details you choose to share, and they decide who to invite.

Do I pay to join?

Creating a profile is free. You pay for your own DBS check, which is a required step. A premium membership is available but optional.

Who sees my phone number and email?

Nobody on the public profile. An employer only sees your full contact details after you accept an invite for a specific role, and access ends when that pipeline closes.

How will I hear about live-in work?

Two ways. Employers can view your profile summary and invite you. If you tick the email option, we also send alerts when a live-in role matches your areas and block lengths. Alerts go as they appear or as a weekly summary, whichever you pick.

Can I stop the emails?

Yes. Every email has a link to your live-in profile, where you can change your frequency or switch alerts off at any time. Employers can still view your profile unless you hide it.

Can I change my rates or locations later?

Yes. Update your profile at any time. Changes apply to future invites only.

Can I work as a live-in carer if I need sponsorship?

Overseas sponsorship for care worker and senior care worker roles closed on 22 July 2025, so new sponsored applicants can't be placed in these roles. If you already hold a Health and Care Worker visa, you may be able to switch employer. Check with an immigration adviser before you apply.
