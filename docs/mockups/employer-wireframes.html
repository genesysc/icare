Two things this sitemap enforces structurally. First, /search and /bookmarks never expose name, photo or contact details — that's not a display choice on this screen, it's that the underlying candidate record simply doesn't carry those fields until an invite tied to a specific job is accepted. Second, /pricing and /billing exist only here — nothing about payment appears anywhere on the candidate-facing domain, by design.

Decided: chat is a hand-off layer, not a rendering surface. It never displays a candidate list, a pipeline board, or a form inline — every substantive result ends in a link to the actual screen (03, 06, 02, etc.) with the relevant filters or context already applied. This keeps exactly one place in the product where candidate results are ever displayed, which is what makes the no-ranking rule enforceable in the first place — a second rendering path in chat would be a second place that rule could quietly get broken.

Conversation history sits in its own nested rail, the same pattern as ChatGPT or Claude — past conversations are named, listed, and reopenable, distinct from the app's main nav rail on the left of it.

Decided: memory is shared across the organisation's team seats, not scoped to the individual recruiter. If two people use the same Beechwood House account, one person's search history and suggestions carry over to the other. Worth a visible "who searched this" trace in the conversation history sidebar so a shared thread doesn't feel anonymous or confusing to whoever didn't start it.

Guardrail that still holds: memory can change how fast chat resolves a query and what it proactively suggests to the recruiter. It never changes which candidates are shown or in what order once handed off — that screen's own unranked, plain-filter logic is untouched by anything chat remembers.

A job record is what an invite is tied to, and what a pipeline is scoped against — so its fields need to be exactly what shows up later on the candidate's invite screen (role, employer, setting, hours, location).

Notes

These fields are the ones an invite later displays verbatim on the candidate side. Role title, setting, location, hours — whatever's captured here is what a candidate reads on iCare's invite detail screen, so copy consistency matters between the two domains even though they're separately built.

The sponsorship restriction is enforced here at the point of job creation, not left to a compliance check later — the field disables itself for the two closed role types rather than accepting the input and rejecting it after the fact.

Action item, not a design question: the exact wording of the three sponsorship options (no sponsorship / can sponsor an existing visa holder switching employer / can sponsor a new applicant) still needs sign-off from an immigration or employment lawyer before this ships. Getting the phrasing wrong here risks misleading candidates or exposing iCare to compliance risk — this is a review to schedule, not something the wireframe can resolve.

New on the candidate side: candidates now declare their own sponsorship need at registration and can update it any time (see icareltd.com screen 07). This job-level field states what the employer can offer for this specific role; the candidate's field states what they need. They're separate settings, not the same field shown twice.

Notes

We don't claim to verify DBS certificates anywhere on this screen. If a DBS line appears here at all, it must use one of the three agreed states and never the word "Verified" or "clean" — same rule as the candidate side.

"Send invite" only appears scoped to a job. There's no generic "contact this candidate" action — every invite is tied to the specific job record selected in Search, which is what makes the consent and the eventual pipeline meaningful.

Decided: this must match the candidate's own preview exactly, field for field. Not a nice-to-have — any drift between what iCare shows a candidate ("this is what employers see") and what iRecruit actually shows an employer is a broken promise, not a cosmetic inconsistency. Both screens should likely be generated from one shared redaction rule, not built twice by two separate teams interpreting the same spec.

The employer-side mirror of the candidate's consent screen. Confirms exactly what the candidate will be told is at stake, so an employer can't send an invite without seeing the same consequence language the candidate sees.

Notes

Decided elsewhere, reflected here: 7-day expiry, and the employer is notified either way — on timeout, or on an active decline, which always carries a reason from a fixed six-option list (down to "prefer not to say," which is itself a complete answer).

Decided: the reason appears verbatim on this invite record. When a candidate declines, the employer sees the actual reason selected — not an aggregated or anonymised summary — directly on this same invite, once it moves to Declined status.
