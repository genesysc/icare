Two things this sitemap asserts. First, /pipelines is separate from /invites — an invite is a decision you make once, a pipeline is a state you sit in afterwards, and merging them makes the consent moment ambiguous. Second, onboarding is findable from step 3, so steps 4–7 are resumable rather than gates.

Notes

Three actions, not two. "Decide later" prevents a forced binary on a screen someone opened on a break. Without it, the safe action is to close the app, and the invite quietly expires.

Decided: declining always sends a reason, and the employer always sees it. A short preset list — not looking right now, pattern doesn't work, too far, wrong role, already accepted elsewhere, or "prefer not to say." That last option is a complete, valid answer on its own; there's no way to decline with literally nothing shared.

This settles the earlier open question directly: a decline is never mistakeable for a timeout on the employer's side. Expiry (7 days, no response) notifies the employer that the window lapsed; an active decline notifies them with a reason. The two events now read differently by design, which is the more honest outcome for both sides.

Decided: the reason list is fixed and identical for every profession and role type — no per-role variants, no distance field bolted onto "too far to travel." One consistent list keeps the decline screen simple and comparable across the whole workforce.

Decided: "Prefer not to say" is recorded internally, never shown to employers. After 5 consecutive "prefer not to say" declines from the same candidate, iCare sends the candidate a gentle nudge — not surfaced to any employer — and offers a direct option, right there, to turn off discoverability entirely if they'd rather not be found by employers at all right now.

Notes

Decided: the six pipeline stages. Shortlisted → Invited for Interview → Pending Interview Result → Successful / Rejected → Onboarding. "Successful" and "Rejected" are both terminal outcomes of the interview stage; "Onboarding" is a further stage reached only from Successful, not a synonym for it.

Deferred for later fine-tuning, per instruction: what "Shortlisted" actually requires from the employer (is it just accepting the invite, or a separate action?), what "Onboarding" contains as its own sub-steps, and whether "Pending Interview Result" needs its own expected-by date the way invites do. The six names are locked; the mechanics behind each are not.

This preview is also the honest answer to "what are you doing with my data" — it's worth linking to it from the landing page and from onboarding, not just from the profile.

Decided: no promotion pipeline across the board. Verifying every employment entry for every candidate isn't workable — people can hold several roles in a short period, and checking each one doesn't scale. An entry keeps whatever grade it naturally earns (Derived from dates the candidate entered, Declared from their own description, Evidenced if they upload something like a payslip) and there's no expectation it moves further.

If they choose to, and iCare confirms with that referee, the associated entry can show as Verified — a real differentiator, since employers may prefer candidates whose referees are already confirmed, but never a requirement to be found or hired.

Notes

DBS is a status, not a badge. It sits in its own block with its own wording rather than taking one of the four grades, because "Verified" as a chip next to DBS is exactly the claim that can't be made.

Decided: DBS status is confirmed by iCare staff manually, not a live DBS Update Service API integration. Copy on this screen should say "we'll confirm within [X] working days" rather than implying an instant check — worth pinning down the actual turnaround time before this ships, since that number is now a real promise to candidates.

Decided: reference checks are conducted by iCare directly, not left to the employer or self-declared by the candidate. This affects the Experience section on the profile too — a reference confirmed by iCare is presumably what moves an entry from Derived or Declared up to Evidenced or Verified, though the exact grading mechanics per entry still need defining (see screen 06 notes on employment history badges).

Documents uploaded here need a visible retention answer: who can see them, and when they're deleted. Data minimisation was a stated non-negotiable and this is where it's tested.

New: candidates declare their own sponsorship need at registration (onboarding step 1), and can change it any time from here — the example given was someone moving to part-time work once already sponsored by another employer. This is a separate field from the employer-side sponsorship offering on a job posting (iRecruit screen 02); one states what a candidate needs, the other states what an employer can provide for a specific role. Search and invite matching presumably compares the two, though that matching logic hasn't been specified yet.

Notes

Two tiers of control, deliberately. Fields the person governs, and a fixed set that's hidden pre-acceptance regardless — because if name and photo were user-toggleable, the consent model stops being a guarantee and becomes a default.

Decided: "Public" means other iCare members only — never the open indexed web. Worth stating this plainly on the screen itself, not just relying on the person already knowing, since for healthcare staff who've had a safeguarding concern or left a role badly, that distinction is genuinely consequential.

Decided: no per-employer blocking. Appearing in a search costs a candidate nothing on its own — full identity is never shared unless they explicitly accept an invite. Since a named employer can't see who a candidate is just by finding them, there's nothing for blocking to protect against that the accept/decline consent step doesn't already cover. This control is out of scope.

Notes

Following an organisation is not the same as being findable by it. Worth keeping those visibly separate, or people will assume following a care home opts them into its searches.

Connections raise a question the recruitment side doesn't. If a person's connections are visible, a colleague can infer they're job-hunting from who they've connected with. LinkedIn has this problem and handles it badly. Worth deciding early rather than inheriting it.

If Network ships in phase 2, the tab bar becomes Home · Invites · Pipelines · Profile, which is a more honest launch product anyway.
