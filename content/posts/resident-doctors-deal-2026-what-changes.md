Three years of strikes ended with a vote in June 2026.

In September, the consequences started showing up in payslips and contracts.

It would be easy to file this under "pay dispute, resolved". That would miss the more interesting half of the story. The parts of this deal that will shape medical careers over the next decade aren't about the headline percentage at all. They're about who gets a permanent job.

## The pay, in plain numbers

The government accepted a **3.5% headline award** for resident doctors for 2026/27, backdated to 1 April 2026. But changes to the pay structure mean some doctors see larger increases.

Reported figures for basic pay include:

- **FY1:** from £38,831 to £41,226, around **6.2%**
- **FY2:** from £44,439 to £47,610, around **7.1%**
- **ST1/CT1:** from £52,656 to £55,355, around **5.1%**

NHS England's [guidance to commissioners and providers](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/2026-27-nhs-pay-awards-revenue-finance-and-contracting-guidance/) confirms the pay reforms are implemented in staff pay from September 2026 and backdated to April.

Beyond basic pay, the offer set out in the [government's June 2026 document](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/government-offer-to-resident-doctors-june-2026/offer-to-bma-uk-resident-doctors-committee-june-2026-accessible-version) and NHS England's [explainer](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/understanding-the-offer-to-resident-doctors/) includes reimbursement of eligible royal college membership, portfolio and exam fees, and an increase in clinical academic pay premia.

For doctors who have spent years paying for exams out of taxed income, fee reimbursement is not a footnote.

## The bigger story: locally employed doctors

For years, locally employed doctors (LEDs) have been the NHS's invisible workforce. Employed directly by trusts outside formal training programmes, they've often worked on local contracts with varying terms, uncertain progression and fixed-term rolling insecurity.

Three changes are aimed squarely at that.

**1. Substantive contracts.** From August 2026, employers are expected to transition eligible locally employed doctors onto substantive contracts, unless there is a legitimate reason for a fixed term, such as covering maternity leave, sickness or a secondment.

**2. Contracts mirroring the 2016 terms.** From September, LEDs are expected to be offered contracts mirroring the 2016 terms and conditions for doctors in training, while national standard contracts are developed.

**3. A permanent route into SAS roles.** From **1 September 2026**, eligible LEDs working in roles comparable to specialty and specialist (SAS) posts can express interest in moving to a permanent SAS contract. The initial choice window runs to **March 2027**. Eligibility includes at least 24 months of continuous employment with the same employer in a comparable role, and employers are expected to make eligible doctors aware and support competency verification. NHS Employers has published the [eligibility and permanency framework](https://www.nhsemployers.org/publications/sas-eligibility-and-permanency-framework).

**4. Enhanced appraisals that count.** An enhanced appraisal process is being developed to let eligible doctors evidence competencies gained outside formal training and use them towards pay progression. NHS Employers expects the framework to be developed by October 2026.

Taken together, that's a serious attempt to turn temporary medical work into recognisable careers.

## Why this is a retention story, not just a pay story

Here's a truth that workforce plans keep rediscovering: **you cannot recruit your way out of a retention problem.**

Every experienced doctor who leaves takes years of training with them and creates a vacancy that costs money and time to fill, often with agency cover in the meantime. Retention is the cheapest recruitment there is.

The deal tries to address the things that actually make people stay: secure contracts, recognition of skills, a visible route upwards, and support with the costs of professional development.

It arrives alongside new national staff standards for NHS employers introduced earlier in 2026, and ahead of the [much-delayed 10 Year Workforce Plan](/blog/nhs-10-year-workforce-plan-delay).

## What to do if you're a doctor

**If you're in training:** check that your September pay reflects the reformed pay point and the backdated award, and claim the fees you're now entitled to have reimbursed. Keep the receipts.

**If you're a locally employed doctor:** this is your window. Find out whether your employer considers you eligible for a substantive contract, ask about the SAS choice window before March 2027, and start assembling evidence of your competencies now. Employers are expected to support verification, but nobody will build your portfolio for you.

**If you're considering leaving the NHS:** the terms you were weighing up may have changed. It's worth rechecking the offer before making a permanent decision.

## What to do if you employ doctors

**Communicate eligibility proactively.** Employers are expected to make eligible doctors aware of the SAS process. Doing that early, clearly and in writing avoids disputes later and signals that you value the people already working for you.

**Plan the verification capacity.** Competency assessment takes senior time. A six-month window closes quickly if nobody is freed up to do the assessments.

**Treat this as a recruitment advantage.** In a tight market for experienced doctors, being the employer known for converting LEDs to permanent contracts quickly is a genuine differentiator, in the same way that [taking on newly qualified clinicians](/blog/newly-qualified-nurses-physios-no-jobs-2026) builds loyalty others miss.

## The honest caveat

Contracts change faster than culture.

A permanent contract doesn't fix rota gaps, supervision quality or the experience of moving trust every few months. The BMA itself framed the agreement as a step forward rather than a solution.

Implementation is being worked through by joint delivery groups involving NHS Employers, DHSC, NHS England and the BMA, and the detail will keep evolving. Check the current guidance before acting on any specific point.

## The bottom line

The headline was pay. The legacy may be contracts.

For thousands of locally employed doctors, September 2026 marked the start of a route from insecure local arrangements to permanent NHS careers with recognised progression.

If that's you, the window is open until March 2027. Don't let it close while you wait for someone to hand you a form.
