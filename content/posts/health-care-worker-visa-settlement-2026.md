> **Please note:** This article is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Rules change often. Always check [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/health-care-worker-visa) or speak to a regulated immigration adviser before making decisions.

Few areas of health and care policy have moved as quickly, or caused as much anxiety, as immigration.

In just over a year, the UK closed a recruitment route that had supported hundreds of thousands of care workers, proposed tripling the time some of them wait for permanent residence, and then entered a long silence while it decided what to do.

If you employ international staff, or you are one, the uncertainty is exhausting.

So let's separate what's settled from what isn't.

## What has already changed

### 1. The care worker route is closed to new overseas applicants

On **22 July 2025**, new overseas sponsorship for care worker and senior care worker roles closed. Providers can no longer bring in new care workers from abroad on this route.

There is a transitional window. People already in the UK can extend their stay or switch to a new sponsoring employer until **22 July 2028**, subject to the rules.

### 2. The skills threshold went back up

The Skilled Worker route's skill threshold returned to degree level (RQF 6), following the 2025 immigration white paper. That's why the care worker route, which sat below that level, was closed.

### 3. Dependants were restricted earlier

Since March 2024, most new care workers and senior care workers have not been able to bring dependants, with limited exceptions for people already on the route before that date.

### 4. Clinical roles remain open

The Health and Care Worker visa is still available for eligible **registered clinical professionals**, such as nurses, doctors and allied health professionals. This is a crucial distinction that often gets lost in headlines.

## The impact is already visible

Skills for Care's latest data shows only around **30,000 new international workers** joined adult social care directly from abroad in 2025/26, the lowest figure in four years, as [National Health Executive reported](https://www.nationalhealthexecutive.com/articles/adult-social-care-vacancy-rate-falls-decade-low-challenges-persist). At the same time, the number of British workers in the sector fell.

We explore what that means for staffing in our breakdown of [the 2026 social care vacancy rate](/blog/social-care-vacancy-rate-2026).

## What hasn't been decided: earned settlement

In November 2025, the Home Office published proposals for a new system called **earned settlement**.

Under the [consultation](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/earned-settlement/a-fairer-pathway-to-settlement-statement-and-accompanying-consultation-on-earned-settlement-accessible), the standard wait for indefinite leave to remain (ILR) would rise from five years to a 10-year baseline, with reductions for factors like higher earnings and increases for others.

The most controversial part: the government consulted on a **15-year baseline** for workers classed as medium-skilled and below. As the [Home Affairs Committee](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5901/cmselect/cmhaff/1409/report.html) noted, that would include most care workers.

A [Lancet Regional Health – Europe commentary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12861310/) estimated the proposals could affect around 616,000 overseas health and care workers who came to the UK between 2022 and 2024.

The consultation closed on 12 February 2026.

### Where things stand now

As of the Greater Manchester Immigration Aid Unit's [August 2026 briefing](https://gmiau.org/earned-settlement-august-2026/), no changes to the length of settlement routes had been made for work visas. There have been media reports of a possible exemption for current care workers, but nothing had been confirmed at the time of writing.

**Bottom line for now: the five-year route has not been replaced. But it is under review, and the final shape is unknown.**

We will update this article when a decision is published.

## What international workers can do

**1. Protect your continuous residence.**
Whatever system applies, gaps and breaches can reset or damage your eligibility. Keep your employment, address and travel records tidy.

**2. Update your visa when you change jobs.**
If you move employer, change occupation code, or your salary or hours fall below the level your permission was granted on, you usually need to [apply to update your visa](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/health-and-care-visa/). Don't assume a new contract is automatically covered.

**3. Keep your professional record strong.**
Qualifications, training, references and a clean compliance history all support future applications, and they make you more attractive to the employers who can still sponsor.

**4. Get advice from a regulated professional.**
Immigration advice in the UK is regulated. Use a solicitor or an adviser registered with the relevant regulator, not social media groups.

## What employers can do

**1. Audit your sponsored workforce now.**
Know exactly who is on transitional permission, when their leave expires, and what 22 July 2028 means for each person.

**2. Be precise about what you can offer.**
There is a big difference between "can sponsor a new applicant", "can sponsor someone already on a Health and Care Worker visa who is switching employer", and "cannot sponsor". Vague adverts waste everyone's time and can mislead candidates. (It's why iCare's job records use a structured three-option sponsorship field instead of a free-text box.)

**3. Stay on top of sponsor duties.**
Record-keeping, reporting changes, and paying at least the required salary are not optional. Official figures show [greater scrutiny of sponsors and enforcement action](https://garthcoates.com/health-and-care-worker-visa-changes/) against non-compliant employers.

**4. Build a domestic pipeline alongside.**
For care worker roles, international recruitment from abroad is no longer an option. Invest in local recruitment, training and retention as your primary strategy, and use the [Care Workforce Pathway](/blog/social-care-fair-pay-agreement-care-workforce-pathway) to show candidates where the role leads.

## The bottom line

The rules for international health and care workers are in their most unsettled state in years.

Some things are fixed: the care worker route is closed to new overseas applicants, the transitional window runs to July 2028, and clinical roles remain open. The biggest question, how long people will wait for settlement, is still open.

Until it's answered, the best strategy for workers and employers is the same: keep records clean, understand exactly which rules apply to you, and don't make long-term decisions on the strength of a headline.
