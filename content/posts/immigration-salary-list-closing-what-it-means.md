On 31 December 2026, the Immigration Salary List (ISL) closes.

For many sponsored workers, nothing changes. For some, a lot does. And the difference comes down to one date.

This post explains who is affected, why one date matters so much, and what to do with the time left.

## The short version

If you are sponsored in a role below degree level (RQF levels 3 to 5) that relies on the ISL, your options after 31 December 2026 depend on **when your Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS) was first assigned**.

That is the date that counts. Not when you applied. Not when your visa was granted.

Roles in this group include healthcare support workers in nursing auxiliary and assistant roles (SOC 6131), pharmaceutical technicians (SOC 3212) and some laboratory technicians (SOC 3111). The full list of roles is on [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/skilled-worker-visa-immigration-salary-list).

## Two paths, one date

<h3 id="path-before">If your CoS was first assigned before 22 July 2025</h3>
<p>You are covered by transitional arrangements. You can keep applying for:</p>
<ul>
<li>visa extensions</li>
<li>changes of employment</li>
<li>settlement</li>
</ul>
<p>These applications continue to use the £25,000 salary threshold, or any updated equivalent if it changes.</p>

<section class="path path-after" aria-labelledby="path-after">
<p class="path-label">Path 2</p>
<h3 id="path-after">If your CoS was first assigned on or after 22 July 2025</h3>
<p>The picture is different.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Until 31 December 2026,</strong> you can still extend your permission or change employment while the ISL remains in place.</li>
<li><strong>After 31 December 2026,</strong> you will no longer be able to apply for an extension or a change of employment in an RQF 3-5 role.</li>
<li>To stay on the route, any future extension or change of employer would need to be in a role at <strong>RQF level 6 or above</strong>.</li>
<li>Settlement remains available using the £25,000 salary threshold, or any updated equivalent.</li>
</ul>
</section>

## What does not change

Some things are worth stating plainly, because they get lost in the noise.

- **RQF level 6 roles are not affected in this way.** Workers sponsored at RQF 6 can keep applying for extensions, changes of employment and settlement, whenever they were first sponsored.
- **Applications can still be made after 31 December** where a valid CoS was assigned before the list closed.
- **Settlement is still available under the current rules.**

## What is still uncertain

Two honest caveats.

First, the Home Office has said it has no additional published guidance on this at the moment. More detail may follow.

Second, all of this reflects today's immigration rules. The government's [earned settlement proposals](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/earned-settlement) could change both the qualifying period and the criteria for settlement. We covered those in our [wider guide to health and care visas and settlement](/blog/health-care-worker-visa-settlement-2026), and we will post updates as soon as there is news.

## Three questions to ask yourself this week

You cannot control what the Home Office decides. You can control how prepared you are.

1. **When was my CoS first assigned?** Find the date. Check your CoS, your contract or ask your employer's HR team.
2. **What RQF level is my role?** Ask your employer which level and SOC code your sponsorship uses.
3. **Do I need to extend or change employer before 31 December?** If you are in the "on or after 22 July 2025" group, the answer decides how much time you have.

Write the answers down. Then keep your evidence in order: payslips, contracts, English test results and right-to-work records. Every future application gets easier when your paperwork is ready.

## For employers

If you sponsor healthcare staff, it is worth identifying who in your team falls into the "on or after 22 July 2025" group, and planning for roles where an extension or change of employment may be needed after the closure date. The CoS assignment date is the one to check.
