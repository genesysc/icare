Picture two headlines from the same industry, published eight months apart.

**January 2026:** Nearly half of the UK's largest pharmaceutical companies expect to cut UK jobs this year.

**September 2026:** The UK pharmaceutical industry sees green shoots, with £2 billion of new investment and a path to tens of thousands of new jobs.

Both are true. Both come from the same trade body.

If you work in life sciences, or you're thinking about it, understanding how those two stories fit together is the key to making smart career decisions right now.

## Story one: a turbulent 2025

The UK had a difficult 2025 for pharmaceutical investment.

The Association of the British Pharmaceutical Industry's [investment competitiveness report](https://www.abpi.org.uk/publications/uk-pharmaceutical-investment-competitiveness-report-2026/) describes several major disinvestment decisions made through that year. A key factor was the clawback rate companies pay on sales of newer branded medicines under the VPAG scheme, which peaked at a minimum of 22.9% in 2025.

The impact on jobs showed up in the ABPI's [Medicines Impacts and Investment Survey](https://www.abpi.org.uk/publications/a-year-of-uncertainty-uk-life-sciences-investment-and-medicine-launches-in-2025/), published in January 2026:

- Among firms with VPAG-measured sales above £100 million, **9 of 21 expected to reduce UK headcount** in 2026. Only 2 expected to expand.
- Those firms account for **93%** of the full-time roles captured in the survey.
- **83%** of all firms said VPAG levels were an essential factor in future headcount planning.

For people working in UK affiliate teams, that translated into real uncertainty.

## Story two: green shoots in 2026

By September 2026, the tone had shifted.

The ABPI's latest report points to a series of policy changes:

- The clawback rate for newer medicines was **capped at 15%**, and the 2026 rate fell to 14.5%.
- The NICE cost-effectiveness threshold was raised in April 2026, the first increase since it was introduced in the early 2000s. Nine additional medicines were approved in the following three months.
- The government committed to double investment in innovative medicines to 0.6% of GDP over the next decade.

Since September 2025, the ABPI says the global industry has committed **£2 billion** of new investment into the UK.

And the potential upside is large. The industry supports more than **125,000 jobs** today. The ABPI estimates it could create up to **81,300 new jobs by 2035** if growth accelerates, a 64% increase that would take the workforce to around 200,000.

So far, so hopeful. Now for the catch.

## The catch: who is going to fill those jobs?

Jobs only get created where the right people exist to do them. And the ABPI's own data flags cracks in the talent pipeline.

### 1. Most roles need a degree

**80%** of UK pharmaceutical job postings require a degree or higher. This is a knowledge-intensive industry, and it doesn't take shortcuts on qualifications.

### 2. The UK is producing proportionally fewer science graduates

The share of UK students graduating in natural sciences, mathematics or statistics fell from **9.22% in 2020 to 7.97% in 2023**. That dropped the UK from 1st to 6th among the countries the ABPI compares.

The report also notes that **11 universities have closed undergraduate chemistry courses** due to financial pressures.

### 3. Apprenticeship routes have narrowed

Apprenticeship Levy funding for most postgraduate (Level 7) apprenticeships was removed in January 2026. The ABPI had [argued for retaining some Level 7 apprenticeships](https://www.abpi.org.uk/media/blogs/2025/february/growth-and-skills-levy-a-catalyst-for-the-uk-pharmaceutical-industry/) aligned to growth sectors like life sciences.

### 4. Hiring from abroad is expensive

The UK's Skilled Worker visa cost rose 4.3% in 2025 to **more than £12,000 per person**, the highest of the 13 countries in the ABPI's comparison.

The government is piloting expanded access to the Global Talent visa for ABPI members and other R&D-intensive businesses. But the ABPI warns the benefit could be undermined if settlement timelines for talent and their dependants are pulled apart. That links directly to the unresolved [earned settlement proposals](/blog/health-care-worker-visa-settlement-2026).

## Clinical research: a pipeline problem you can see

Clinical trials are where many pharmaceutical careers begin, and where the skills pressure is visible.

The ABPI reports that recruitment to interventional industry clinical trials in the UK **fell by 25%** between 2022/23 and 2024/25. There has been progress on approvals, with 98% of trials in April 2026 receiving regulatory approval within 60 days. Later stages still lag: only 58% of trials opened to recruitment within 60 days of approval.

Industry clinical trials support around 65,000 UK jobs, according to the report. Getting set-up and recruitment right is as much a workforce question as a regulatory one: research nurses, coordinators, pharmacists and data specialists all have to be in place.

## What this means for your career

**If you're a science student or graduate:**
Demand for degree-level skills is structural, not temporary. Chemistry, biology, data science and bioinformatics all feed into the industry. Combining a science foundation with digital or data skills is a strong position.

**If you're a pharmacist:**
Pharma is one of several paths open to you. Medical affairs, regulatory, pharmacovigilance and clinical research roles value clinical knowledge. With [new pharmacists now qualifying as independent prescribers](/blog/pharmacist-independent-prescribers-2026), the range of patient-facing options is also growing.

**If you're a nurse or allied health professional:**
Clinical research delivery needs registered clinicians. Research nurse and trial coordinator roles can be a route into life sciences without leaving patient care behind.

**If you work in pharma now:**
Keep your professional profile current, and build skills that travel between companies and functions. In an industry where investment decisions can shift within a year, portable expertise is your best protection.

## What employers can do

The ABPI's data makes one thing clear: investment follows talent.

Companies that want to grow in the UK can't rely only on graduate intake or international hiring. Structured apprenticeships at lower levels, partnerships with universities, and pathways for clinicians moving from the NHS into industry all widen the pool.

## The bottom line

UK pharma is moving from a year of retreat towards a year of cautious recovery. The investment is starting to come back.

But jobs don't create themselves. The industry's ability to turn £2 billion of commitments into tens of thousands of careers depends on something slower and harder than pricing policy: a steady supply of skilled people.

For anyone considering a life sciences career, that's the most encouraging signal of all. The industry needs you more than the headlines suggest.
